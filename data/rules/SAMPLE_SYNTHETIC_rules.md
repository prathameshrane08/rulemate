## Chapter II-2 - Fire safety and escape (synthetic)

### Regulation 9 - Containment of fire

1 Purpose

1.1 The purpose of this regulation is to contain a fire in the space of origin. For this purpose, the ship shall be subdivided by thermal and structural boundaries as set out in 2 and 3.

2 Main vertical zones

2.1 The hull, superstructure and deckhouses of passenger ships shall be subdivided into main vertical zones by A-60 class divisions.

2.2 The mean length of a main vertical zone shall not exceed 48 m on any deck.

2.3 Steps and recesses in main vertical zone bulkheads shall be kept to a minimum and shall be of A-60 class. Openings in these bulkheads shall be protected by fire doors complying with 3.1.

3 Fire doors

3.1 Fire doors in main vertical zone bulkheads shall have a fire integrity rating of not less than 60 min and shall be of A-60 class.

3.2 Fire doors in stairway enclosures shall have a fire integrity rating of not less than 30 min. See Table 9.1 for the full list of door locations.

<!-- page: 2 -->

3.3 Fire doors shall be self-closing. The closing time shall be not more than 40 s and not less than 10 s from the beginning of their movement, with the ship in the upright position.

3.4 Fire doors held open by hold-back hooks shall be capable of release from a continuously manned central control station. After release, the closing requirements of 3.3 apply.

3.5 Fire doors shall be capable of closing against an inclination of up to 3.5 degrees opposing closure.

Table 9.1 - Minimum fire integrity of doors (synthetic)

| Door location | Division class | Minimum fire integrity (min) | Self-closing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Main vertical zone bulkhead | A-60 | 60 | Yes |
| Stairway enclosure | A-30 | 30 | Yes |
| Galley boundary | A-30 | 30 | Yes |
| Cabin to corridor | B-15 | 15 | No |

Note: Where a door location is not listed in Table 9.1, the requirement shall be agreed with the Administration.

### Regulation 13 - Means of escape

1 Purpose

1.1 The purpose of this regulation is to provide means of escape so that persons on board can safely and swiftly escape to the lifeboat and liferaft embarkation deck.

2 General requirements

2.1 Unless expressly provided otherwise in this regulation, at least two widely separated and ready means of escape shall be provided from all spaces or groups of spaces.

2.2 Lifts shall not be considered as forming one of the means of escape required by this regulation.

2.3 Escape routes shall be marked and lit as required in 6.

<!-- page: 3 -->

3 Escape from passenger and crew spaces

3.1 Corridors

3.1.1 The clear width of corridors serving as escape routes shall be not less than 1200 mm.

3.1.2 In crew accommodation, the clear width of corridors may be reduced to not less than 900 mm, provided that the corridor serves not more than 50 persons.

3.1.3 Dead-end corridors shall not exceed 7 m in length.

3.1.4 Where a corridor serves more persons than its minimum width allows, the width shall be calculated in accordance with 4.2.

3.2 Stairways

3.2.1 Stairways serving as escape routes shall have an angle of inclination not exceeding 45 degrees to the horizontal.

3.2.2 The clear width of stairways shall be not less than 900 mm. Stairways wider than 1800 mm shall be fitted with an intermediate handrail as required by 3.2.4.

3.2.3 A stairway shall not have a vertical rise of more than 3.5 m without a landing.

3.2.4 Handrails shall be fitted on both sides of stairways at a height of not less than 850 mm and not more than 1000 mm above the stair nosing.

3.2.5 The riser height of stairs shall not exceed 200 mm and the tread depth shall be not less than 240 mm.

3.3 Escape route length

3.3.1 The travel distance from any point in a passenger cabin to the nearest stairway enclosure shall not exceed 40 m.

3.3.2 Where the escape route passes through a public space with a floor area of more than 150 m2, the travel distance in 3.3.1 may be increased to 50 m, subject to the approval of the Administration.

3.3.3 Escape routes shall not pass through galleys, machinery spaces or store rooms.

4 Calculation of escape route widths

4.1 General

4.1.1 The widths of escape routes shall be based on the number of persons expected to use them. Minimum values are given in Table 13.1.

4.2 Corridors

4.2.1 The clear width of corridors shall be not less than 10 mm per person served, and in no case less than the minimum width given in 3.1.1.

4.2.2 Where the number of persons served by a corridor exceeds 150, the corridor shall be divided into two separate escape routes.

4.3 Stairways

4.3.1 The clear width of stairways shall be not less than 10 mm per person served, and in no case less than the minimum width given in 3.2.2.

Table 13.1 - Minimum clear widths of escape routes (synthetic)

| Escape route element | Minimum clear width (mm) | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Passenger corridor | 1200 | 3.1.1 |
| Crew corridor, not more than 50 persons | 900 | 3.1.2 |
| Stairway | 900 | 3.2.2 |
| Door in escape route | 800 | 5.2 |
| Cabin door | 700 | 5.2 |

5 Doors in escape routes

5.1 Doors in escape routes shall in general open in the direction of escape. Cabin doors are exempt from this requirement.

5.2 The clear width of doors in escape routes shall be not less than 800 mm. Cabin doors shall have a clear width of not less than 700 mm.

5.3 Doors in escape routes shall be operable by one person, with an opening force not exceeding 100 N.

6 Marking and lighting

6.1 Escape routes, including stairways and exits, shall be marked by low-location lighting at all points of the escape route, including corners and intersections.

6.2 The low-location lighting shall remain operational for at least 60 min after activation.

### Regulation 14 - Evacuation analysis

1 Application

1.1 An evacuation analysis shall be carried out early in the design process for passenger ships carrying more than 36 passengers.

2 Criteria

2.1 The total calculated evacuation time shall not exceed 60 min.

2.2 The analysis shall identify congestion points and show that the escape route widths comply with Regulation II-2/13.4.

2.3 Travel distances used in the analysis shall be consistent with regulation 13.3.3 and Table 13.1.
